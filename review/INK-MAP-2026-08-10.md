# The ink map — proposal for Al's ruling

Sheet: **`output/ink-map/index.html`** — every condition on a real set-001
photograph, day and night, contrast printed under each frame. The outstanding
**iteration 5 keep/drop** comparison is the first section of the same sheet.

Nothing is in `app.css`. The map is `review/vibrancy/ink-map.css`, the night
option is `review/vibrancy/ink-map-night.css`, and both layer on the kept
vibrancy stack (1–4).

## The proposed map — six inks, five families, thirteen keys

| Family | Ink | Hex | On stock | Conditions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wets | petrol | `#0b5c68` | 6.8:1 | rain · rain-possible · storm · thunder · hail · fog |
| warm | deep pink | `#a81259` | 6.5:1 | heat · uv |
| mild | pen green | `#1c6b3a` | 5.9:1 | clear |
| cold | **violet (new)** | `#3c2f80` | 9.8:1 | cold · cold-clear |
| wind | **umber (new)** | `#5a3a22` | 9.1:1 | wind |
| default | ink-dark | `#1b1813` | 15.8:1 | cloudy |

Lowest ratio in the set is pen green at 5.9:1 — every ink clears 4.5:1 with room,
and the two new candidates are the *highest*-contrast of the six.

**Why cloudy gets the plain black pen.** It is the commonest condition here and
the one with nothing to say. A day the weather has an opinion about gets a
coloured pen; an ordinary one gets the black one — which also means the ink only
"means something" when there is something to mean.

**Why two new candidates rather than four inks stretched over nine conditions.**
Cold and wind have no honest home in wets/warm/mild. A cold clear Karoo morning
inked the same as a Durban heatwave is the map lying to you, and once it lies
once it stops being read. Violet reads cold; umber reads dust and wind.

**The umber question, and how to judge it.** Umber is a brown, not the warning
orange — a third of `#ff9800`'s saturation and half its lightness. You can settle
it directly on the sheet: the wind frame happens to fire the Cape Doctor banner,
so the real warning orange and the umber ink are on screen together. If it still
reads too close, wind folds into petrol and the map is five inks — one line.

## The night option

Same six inks, each mixed 34% toward the page colour: no new hues, no per-slot
variation, and the family you learn in daylight is the family you read at night.
Shot side by side with the flat map in the second column of every row.

One honest consequence: the plain ink barely moves (`#1b1813` → `#191611`),
because it is already nearly the darkest thing on the stock. On a cloudy night
the step is invisible. That is the map telling the truth rather than inventing a
difference.

The hook does not exist in the app yet. If you take the night option, it is one
line in `renderHome`, next to the existing `weather-*` class:

```js
document.body.classList.toggle('tod-night', getTimeOfDay() === 'night');
```

## What the render caught

- **`uv` has no night.** The night frame for uv comes out green, not pink,
  because `computeHomeDisplayCondition` gates the UV branch on `isDay` — after
  dark the app emits `clear`. The sheet labels that row rather than hiding it:
  it is the ink you would really see at 22:00.
- **A prerequisite defect, fixed.** `renderHome` cleared only nine of the
  thirteen hero classes it can stamp — `thunder`, `hail`, `rain-possible` and
  `cold-clear` were missing, so a cold-clear morning followed by a clear
  afternoon left `hero-cold-clear` *and* `hero-clear` on the caption. Harmless
  while those keys had no colour; two inks fighting over one line the moment the
  map lands. Fixed in `assets/app.js` with a test
  (`tests/hero-condition-classes.test.js`) that derives the expected list from
  the function's own return values, so the next new condition key cannot repeat
  it.
