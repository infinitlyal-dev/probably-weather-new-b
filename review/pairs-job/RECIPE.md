# RECIPE — how Probably Weather makes a line and a photo

The brief for every future line and photo, including the timed job's. Written 25 Sept 2026 from Al's own
grades on `review/taste-for-al.html` (`review/taste/taste-ruled.json`; he taps LOVE or MEH, a blank counts
as FINE). His old KEEP rulings were made exhausted, in a very hard stretch — these grades are his taste,
the old keeps are not. Numbers: `data/grades-result.json`; the study behind it: `STUDY.md`.

## 1. The pair method (all 10 pilot pairs: LOVE)

**Joke first. Then a photo made to set the joke up.** The photo is the setup, the line is the punchline,
made as one idea. Never write a line that describes a photo, and never pick a photo to illustrate a line
that already describes it. A pair is judged together; neither half is the other's caption.

## 2. The line

**The rule: about the weather now, with a twist.** Everything else is a guide.

1. **About the weather now** — what it is doing, or doing to you, today. Not the photo, not the calendar
   (no weekday or season openers, no months), not a little scene of people.
2. **One twist:** the weather as a character with intentions; a wild exaggeration; a confession; or an SA
   truth everyone knows.
3. **Say it straight to the reader.** Warm or teasing, the way a dry South African friend would.
4. **Short is a guide, not the rule.** Lines of 10 words or fewer: 60 % LOVE, 17 % MEH. Longer: 35 % LOVE,
   35 % MEH. But a longer line that stays on the weather can be loved (below). Cut vagueness, not the idea.
5. **The recipe score predicts him.** Live lines by recipe score: top quarter 71 % LOVE, then 65 %, 47 %,
   bottom quarter 18 % LOVE and 55 % MEH.

**Gold — lines Al loved** (N = new, written blind to the recipe; the rest were live):
- Weather as a character: "The weather gods are showing off." · "The sky just said something. Nobody's
  arguing." (N) · "The clouds are holding a meeting. Could go either way." (N) · "The wind has opinions and
  it's sharing every one." (N) · "The sun's working overtime today, and billing your nose." (N) · "The frost
  has already started its shift. By dawn the lawn will be glass." · "Bright sun, frozen fingers. The sun's
  here for decoration." (N) · "Cape Town just got a dimmer switch."
- Exaggeration: "Rain so heavy it should pay rent." · "Today's forecast: medium rare." · "UV this high, and
  even the shade wants shade." (N) · "Barefoot to the car is an extreme sport today." (N) · "Your steering
  wheel is a hot plate." · "Your front gate is now the edge of the known world."
- Confession: "Might rain. I've already cancelled everything, just in case." (N) · "The sky is throwing ice.
  Honestly, it feels personal." (N) · "Blanket burrito mode: activated."
- SA truth: "Highveld clouds this heavy by two o'clock don't bluff." (N) · "Hail. Every car owner in Gauteng
  just stood up." (N) · "Rain on a farm isn't weather. It's a salary falling from the sky." · "Africa's sky
  just hits different." · "Almost a braai day." · "The coals aren't going to light themselves."
- Straight to the reader, warm: "Good night, South Africa." · "If you're working today, we feel sorry for
  you." · "Night shift weather: approved." · "One perfect day issued. Non-transferable. Spend it wisely."
  · "Don't even think about driving somewhere." · "Lightning's putting on a show — watch from the window."
- The app's own "probably": "Forecast says maybe. We say probably." · "Washing's still safe. Probably."
- Longer and still on the weather: "Somebody forgot to bring in the washing. The washing has not forgotten."
  · "Somebody's driving home right now with the heater on full and the seat warmer as a bonus." ·
  "Everything you can hear tonight is one owl with strong opinions."
- Also loved: "The sky is the colour of an old Tupperware lid." · "No colours in the sky tonight, just shades of
  calm." · "Thunder rolling in. Inside is the move." · "Maybe rain, maybe not. Classic." · "50/50 on getting wet.
  Like a coin toss." · "Bring a jacket. Or sunscreen. Or both." · "Water consumption: yes. All of it." · "Sunny,
  with a 100% chance of shivering." · "Frost on the lawn. Frost on the bakkie. Frost in the pet bowl."

**Avoid — lines Al marked MEH:**
- **Narrating a scene** (the biggest group): "Three hadedas, two crows, one shivering human at the bird
  feeder." · "Every window switching on is someone deciding: that's enough for today." · "'Five more
  minutes' has been going for forty minutes now. Let them." · "Nobody is getting out. The water has only
  just forgiven us." · "The plan was ten minutes. The noses tapped out after two." · "Coffee in the sun on
  the stoep. The whole reason we live here." · "Last one closing up, please leave the light on. It looks
  lovely." · "Somebody's mom is going to have words. Worth it." · "Sleep is cancelled, but look at that shot."
- **Slogan filler:** "Shoes optional. Attitude mandatory." · "Clouds today, vibes optional."
- **Flat label jokes** (the weather's name plus a pun): "Partly cloudy. Also partly awake." · "Cape Town
  partly cloudy: jacket in the car, always."
- **Orders and hedges with nothing behind them:** "Close it, tie it down, surrender." · "Warm enough to
  leave the window open. Brave enough?" · "Gorgeous, but I trust it zero percent." · "The kettle is working
  harder than anyone today."

**New against old:** the 20 blind new lines: 55 % LOVE, 35 % FINE, 10 % MEH. The best-scoring live lines:
71 % LOVE, 24 % MEH. The new lines were loved less often than the best old ones, but disliked far less.
All 60 live lines: 53 % LOVE, 25 % MEH.

**Standing bans:** no Eskom lines beyond the approved ones; no season or month lines; no describing a
photo; no slang as decoration; no "vibes". Braai plans on weekends only.

## 3. The photo

**The picture comes from the joke (Al, 27 Sept 2026).** His verdict on the job's batches 2–9: "the images
were a massive dissapointment. a Complete and utter fail in creativity. We basically got the same theme and
setup across almost all of them." Every brief had come off one template — a city, a time, a day, one person
in plain clothes reacting in front of a cared-for home — so the joke never shaped the picture. His note on
the pillow line: "Read the line, you could use that as inspiration for the image rather."
- **Only a line with a picture in it gets a photo.** The writer writes the picture WITH the line: the joke's
  own situation, caught by a camera — the washing gone and the pegs still on the line; a minibus taxi in a
  downpour with its indicator on; a pillow flipped to its cold side on a hot night. A mood line with nothing
  to show ("The sky's keeping tonight to itself.") is a good line, but it goes to Al as a line for the bank,
  and no photo is made for it.
- **No template.** No fixed "[city], [time], [day], one [person]". The subject, the place and the camera come
  from the idea. Casting varies across the whole set, and the job keeps the count, instead of every brief
  stamping in one person.
- **Variety, checked before anything is made.** Every idea is compared with every photo already in the app
  (one line per live photo, written once and kept: `review/pairs-job/live-photos.json`), with the pairs
  waiting for Al, and with the rest of its batch; a repeat setup — the same kind of subject doing the same
  kind of thing in the same kind of place — is rejected. **Banned for now:** a person outside squinting into
  the sun; hair or clothes blown about in front of a house; a person standing on a lawn in heat; a person at
  their gate or front door. **Mix the subjects:** objects, animals, cars, kitchens and indoors, groups,
  close-ups, wide shots — a single person too, but not by default.
- **The judge sees the whole batch side by side** and rejects lookalikes, as well as checking each photo alone.

**The rules that stay (Al's):**
1. **The weather is doing something, caught candidly:** to someone (squinting is banned for now; sweating,
   soaked, shivering, bracing, burrowed in) or to something (the washing, the braai, the car, the dog's bowl).
   Not people posing in nice weather. An empty scenic frame is not enough for wind or clear (a palm and a
   flag straining got MEH): the weather must visibly be doing the joke to someone or something specific.
2. **Aspirational settings — Al's ruling (26 Sept 2026), never propose dropping it.** Cared-for homes,
   gardens, suburbs, towns and farms, in real South Africa. No shacks, no poverty or decay, no grit. **The
   stock look is fixed by a candid reaction to the weather, never by a grittier place.** Not posed people
   smiling. Al, rejecting the first P01 photo: "it is for a clear day, that image feels like it is for
   extreme heat. Plus it looks a bit like a shack, and the guy seems to be in blue collar worker clothes.
   And its on a sunday. We want images that are more aspirational in terms of settings." (The pairs job's
   first batch lost two loved lines' photos, M01 and M03, to grit for the same reason.)
3. **Clothes and activity fit the slot's day and time.** Every photo is made for a slot: its weather, its
   weekday (slot 1 = Monday … 7 = Sunday) and its time of day. Weekends are leisure; no work clothes or
   work scenes on a Saturday or Sunday unless the joke needs them. A weekday may show the commute, the
   school run or getting ready for work, in a cared-for setting.
4. **The weather's strength matches the folder.** Clear is pleasant sun — bright, warm, comfortable, a
   green garden, nobody suffering. Heat is heat — 33 °C and up, visibly too hot. Never mix them: a clear
   photo that reads as a heat wave is wrong, and so is a heat photo that reads as a nice day. The same
   holds for cold (wet, grey Cape winter) against cold-clear (dry, bright, frost), and rain against storm.
5. **No waxy AI look.** It must look like a real camera photograph: natural skin with pores and flaws, a
   candid unposed moment, available light, slight grain, true colour. No retouched skin, no glossy sheen,
   no plastic faces. (Al on the first pilot photos: "some of those images look very ai waxy".)
6. **Composed for Home:** the subject in the upper half, calm space below it for the joke. Measured on the ten
   pilot pairs (25 Sept): today's Home writes the joke along the photo's bottom edge; Home D writes it
   55–75 % down the screen, not in the bottom third — a subject that reaches below the middle gets covered on D
   (the boy's legs in P03, the man and his washing in P05). Keep the subject above about 50 %.
7. **Al's casting rules stand:** diverse South African casting, named specifically; no red face-brick by
   default; not coastal or Cape Dutch by default; no romantic couples; no text, signs, logos or number
   plates; a real place drawn right or not drawn at all (no cloned skylines, no invented cities); at night
   and in frost, people small or turned away.

**What his grades add** (40 app photos: 22 LOVE, 11 FINE, 7 MEH):
- Posed people are the surest MEH: 0 of 5 posed photos loved, 3 MEH. Four of his five named photos got
  MEH (the fifth, the fairy-light dinner, FINE).
- A photo can be loved with nobody in it **when the weather itself is the character in a real place**:
  fog over a rugby field, a Karoo cloud, the Milky Way, 38 °C on a dashboard, an egg frying on a bonnet.
  That doesn't work for wind: two empty wind photos (a palm and a flag straining) got MEH, and the one
  with hair blown upright got LOVE. Wind and clear need someone the weather is happening to.
- Strong: fog (4 of 4 LOVE), heat (3 of 4), cold-clear (3 of 4). Weakest: clear (the named ones) and wind.
- Photos high on the photo recipe: 59 % LOVE, 11 % MEH; low: 46 % LOVE, 31 % MEH.
- From his written kills on older batches: looks AI-made, a real place drawn wrong, doesn't make sense, not
  South African-looking, too ideal or too perfect, posed or stock.

## 4. The photo brief (what every brief carries — not a template)

The writer's own picture for the line, in its own words — whatever the joke needs in the frame, and what the
weather is doing to it — then who or what is in it (cast specifically when there are people; "nobody" is
fine), the camera (close-up, medium or wide), and the composition (the subject in the upper half, calm space
below for the joke). Around it the job adds only the rules: the slot's weather at its folder's strength, its
day and time of day, an aspirational setting, candid never posed, real-camera realism (natural skin with
pores and flaws, available light, slight grain, true colour; no retouched skin, no glossy sheen, no plastic
faces), and no text, signs, logos or number plates anywhere. The brief carries the line so the maker knows
the joke; the picture is the joke's situation, and never has the line's words in it.

## 5. Checks before Al sees anything

- The line: about the weather now, a twist, not narrating a scene, not a label pun or slogan, no calendar,
  no Eskom, no photo description (the rubric in `RUBRIC-lines.md`; pass = weather-now + twist).
- The photo: the rules above, by eye at full size; then the Home check — the joke must not sit on the
  subject (the photo check) — and the fold gate.
- The variety check, before any photo is made: the idea against every live photo, the pairs waiting and the
  batch; the banned setups; the batch's mix — at most two of any subject, setting or cast (who is in it), one
  garden, one gate — counted over any five pairs side by side. And the line against every line in the bank for
  its weather and every line the job has written: no near-repeats (Al, 27 Sept 2026: "Where lines share a
  formula ('clocked in', 'arguing with', 'before you', 'knows where you live'), keep only the best one"). The
  job keeps a list of formulas already used up.
- The pairs job's judge sees the whole batch side by side (blind to the lines, told each photo's slot and the
  picture it was meant to show): it says NO to a take that looks like another pair's pick, repeats a photo
  already in the app, shows a banned setup, or does not show its picture — and to a photo that is gritty
  (poverty, decay, litter, graffiti, squalor), not aspirational (not a cared-for home, garden, suburb, town
  or farm), wrong for its day (work clothes or a work scene on a weekend, anything that doesn't suit the
  slot's day and time), or wrong in weather strength (clear that reads as a heat wave, heat that reads as a
  nice day), as well as to one that is waxy or not realistic.
- Only Al's ticks ship. Al checks English and Afrikaans; isiZulu, isiXhosa and Sesotho go through the
  language skills and lang-check.

## Limits

- One sitting: 80 lines and 40 photos. Strong enough to steer, not to rank close calls.
- The 60 live lines on his page all came from the condition bank. The page builder missed the photo-bound
  lines (they are keyed by content hash in this copy), so those were not graded; the MEH pattern above
  (narrating a scene) is the same pattern the study found in them.
