# RECIPE — how Probably Weather makes a line and a photo

The brief for every future line and photo, including the timed job's. Written 25 Sept 2026 from Al's own
grades on `review/taste-for-al.html` (`review/taste/taste-ruled.json`; he taps LOVE or MEH, a blank counts
as FINE). His old KEEP rulings were made exhausted, in a very hard stretch — these grades are his taste,
the old keeps are not. Numbers: `data/grades-result.json`; the study behind it: `STUDY.md`.

## 1. The pair method (all 10 pilot pairs: LOVE)

**Joke first. Then a photo made to set the joke up.** The photo is the setup, the line is the punchline,
made as one idea. Never write a line that describes a photo, and never pick a photo to illustrate a line
that already describes it. A pair is judged together; neither half is the other's caption.

## 2. The line

**The rule: about the weather now, with a twist.** Everything else is a guide.

1. **About the weather now** — what it is doing, or doing to you, today. Not the photo, not the calendar
   (no weekday or season openers, no months), not a little scene of people.
2. **One twist:** the weather as a character with intentions; a wild exaggeration; a confession; or an SA
   truth everyone knows.
3. **Say it straight to the reader.** Warm or teasing, the way a dry South African friend would.
4. **Short is a guide, not the rule.** Lines of 10 words or fewer: 60 % LOVE, 17 % MEH. Longer: 35 % LOVE,
   35 % MEH. But a longer line that stays on the weather can be loved (below). Cut vagueness, not the idea.
5. **The recipe score predicts him.** Live lines by recipe score: top quarter 71 % LOVE, then 65 %, 47 %,
   bottom quarter 18 % LOVE and 55 % MEH.

**Gold — lines Al loved** (N = new, written blind to the recipe; the rest were live):

  arguing." (N) · "The clouds are holding a meeting. Could go either way." (N) · "The wind has opinions and
  it's sharing every one." (N) · "The sun's working overtime today, and billing your nose." (N) · "The frost
  has already started its shift. By dawn the lawn will be glass." · "Bright sun, frozen fingers. The sun's
  here for decoration." (N) · "Cape Town just got a dimmer switch."
- Exaggeration: "Rain so heavy it should pay rent." · "Today's forecast: medium rare." · "UV this high, and

- Straight to the reader, warm: "Good night, South Africa." · "If you're working today, we feel sorry for

- The app's own "probably": "Forecast says maybe. We say probably." · "Washing's still safe. Probably."
- Longer and still on the weather: "Somebody forgot to bring in the washing. The washing has not forgotten."
  · "Somebody's driving home right now with the heater on full and the seat warmer as a bonus." ·

- Also loved: "The sky is the colour of an old Tupperware lid." · "No colours in the sky tonight, just shades of

**Avoid — lines Al marked MEH:**
- **Narrating a scene** (the biggest group): "Three hadedas, two crows, one shivering human at the bird
  feeder." · "Every window switching on is someone deciding: that's enough for today." · "'Five more
  minutes' has been going for forty minutes now. Let them." · "Nobody is getting out. The water has only
  just forgiven us." · "The plan was ten minutes. The noses tapped out after two." · "Coffee in the sun on
  the stoep. The whole reason we live here." · "Last one closing up, please leave the light on. It looks
  lovely." · "Somebody's mom is going to have words. Worth it." · "Sleep is cancelled, but look at that shot."
- **Slogan filler:** "Shoes optional. Attitude mandatory." · "Clouds today, vibes optional."
- **Flat label jokes** (the weather's name plus a pun): "Partly cloudy. Also partly awake." · "Cape Town
  partly cloudy: jacket in the car, always."
- **Orders and hedges with nothing behind them:** "Close it, tie it down, surrender." · "Warm enough to
  leave the window open. Brave enough?" · "Gorgeous, but I trust it zero percent." · "The kettle is working
  harder than anyone today."

**New against old:** the 20 blind new lines: 55 % LOVE, 35 % FINE, 10 % MEH. The best-scoring live lines:
71 % LOVE, 24 % MEH. The new lines were loved less often than the best old ones, but disliked far less.
All 60 live lines: 53 % LOVE, 25 % MEH.

**Standing bans:** no Eskom lines beyond the approved ones; no season or month lines; no describing a
photo; no slang as decoration; no "vibes". Braai plans on weekends only.

## 3. The photo

**The rule (Al's):**
1. **The weather is doing something to someone:** one clear subject reacting to it — squinting, sweating,
   hair blown upright, soaked, shivering, bracing. Not people posing in nice weather.
2. **Specific, lived-in South Africa with character.** Not glossy stock. Not posed people smiling.
3. **No waxy AI look.** It must look like a real camera photograph: natural skin with pores and flaws, a
   candid unposed moment, available light, slight grain, true colour. No retouched skin, no glossy sheen,
   no plastic faces. (Al on the first pilot photos: "some of those images look very ai waxy".)
4. **Composed for Home:** the subject in the upper half, calm space below it for the joke. Measured on the ten
   pilot pairs (25 Sept): today's Home writes the joke along the photo's bottom edge; Home D writes it
   55–75 % down the screen, not in the bottom third — a subject that reaches below the middle gets covered on D
   (the boy's legs in P03, the man and his washing in P05). Keep the subject above about 50 %.
5. **Al's casting rules stand:** diverse South African casting, named specifically; no red face-brick by
   default; not coastal or Cape Dutch by default; no romantic couples; no text, signs, logos or number
   plates; a real place drawn right or not drawn at all (no cloned skylines, no invented cities); at night
   and in frost, people small or turned away.

**What his grades add** (40 app photos: 22 LOVE, 11 FINE, 7 MEH):
- Posed people are the surest MEH: 0 of 5 posed photos loved, 3 MEH. Four of his five named photos got
  MEH (the fifth, the fairy-light dinner, FINE).
- A photo can be loved with nobody in it **when the weather itself is the character in a real place**:
  fog over a rugby field, a Karoo cloud, the Milky Way, 38 °C on a dashboard, an egg frying on a bonnet.
  That doesn't work for wind: two empty wind photos (a palm and a flag straining) got MEH, and the one
  with hair blown upright got LOVE. Wind and clear need someone the weather is happening to.
- Strong: fog (4 of 4 LOVE), heat (3 of 4), cold-clear (3 of 4). Weakest: clear (the named ones) and wind.
- Photos high on the photo recipe: 59 % LOVE, 11 % MEH; low: 46 % LOVE, 31 % MEH.
- From his written kills on older batches: looks AI-made, a real place drawn wrong, doesn't make sense, not
  South African-looking, too ideal or too perfect, posed or stock.

## 4. The photo brief (the shape every brief takes)

> Real camera photograph, vertical 9:16. [Place in South Africa, time of day, the weather in plain
> physical terms.] [One subject, cast specifically: age, who they are, what they wear — plain, no logos.]
> [What the weather is doing to them, caught mid-moment.] [The architecture or landscape, named.]
> Composition: the subject in the upper half; the bottom third calm and empty — [what it is].
> Natural skin with pores and flaws, candid and unposed, available light, slight grain, true colour. No
> retouched skin, no glossy sheen, no plastic faces, not stock. No text, signs, logos or numbers anywhere.

Written after the line, from the line's idea. The brief carries the line so the maker knows the joke it
sets up; the photo never illustrates the line's words.

## 5. Checks before Al sees anything

- The line: about the weather now, a twist, not narrating a scene, not a label pun or slogan, no calendar,
  no Eskom, no photo description (the rubric in `RUBRIC-lines.md`; pass = weather-now + twist).
- The photo: the rules above, by eye at full size; then the Home check — the joke must not sit on the
  subject (the photo check) — and the fold gate.
- Only Al's ticks ship. Al checks English and Afrikaans; isiZulu, isiXhosa and Sesotho go through the
  language skills and lang-check.

## Limits

- One sitting: 80 lines and 40 photos. Strong enough to steer, not to rank close calls.
- The 60 live lines on his page all came from the condition bank. The page builder missed the photo-bound
  lines (they are keyed by content hash in this copy), so those were not graded; the MEH pattern above
  (narrating a scene) is the same pattern the study found in them.
