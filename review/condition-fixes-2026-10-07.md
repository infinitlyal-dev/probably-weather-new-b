## 1. Week-strip day cards read the capped cloud figure

The day cards now decide their sky on the same capped cloud number as the hero, read at noon. A day of thin high cirrus no longer shows Cloudy in the week strip while the hero says partly cloudy. At noon on day 0, across six airports: the day card said Cloudy on 101 days; on 75 of them the airport reported no grey cloud. After the fix: 33 Cloudy cards, 14 of them wrong. The cost is the same one you accepted for the hero: 6 days where the airport did see grey cloud and the card now says partly cloudy (Cape Town 17 Jul and 15 Aug, Durban 1 Aug and 21 Aug, George 17 and 18 Jul), against 61 days fixed. Nothing else moved: rain, wind and the hero are unchanged to the decimal. Days 2–6 have no cloud split from Open-Meteo and are unchanged. Tables: `review/accuracy/results/compare-daycard-before-vs-daycard-after.md`.

## 2. "Might rain" from the day's 50 % now reads the next 8 hours, and fog stands

The phone used to say "Might rain." all day whenever the whole day's rain chance was 50 % or more, even when that rain was already over or was 12 hours away: rain actually followed within 12 hours only 44 % of the time. Now it says so only when the next 4 hours are dry and one of the 4 hours after that is 50 % or more. Rain followed that within 8 hours 81 % of the time. I picked 8 hours because it is "the next few hours" and it held up best on the airports. A fog call from the server now stands over it. Across six airports: 716 false "might rain" hours gone (phone might-rain hours 2,266 → 1,505); 76 fog calls no longer overwritten; the cost is 28 hours where it did rain and the hero now shows nothing wet (rain missed 21.4 % → 24.9 % of 800 rain hours), spread over 12 airport-days, against 86 days better. The "Rain later" wording in the stats row follows the same rule. Server, rain-now and wind unchanged. Replay case: Cape Town 16 Sept 08:00 — a 69.5 % day whose rain was over, "Might rain." all morning under scattered cloud. Tables: `review/accuracy/results/compare-later-before-vs-later-after.md`.

## 3. "Rain's here." from Tomorrow.io's radar now needs a second source

The radar still makes "Rain's here.", but only when at least one other source (Open-Meteo, WeatherAPI, Pirate or MET Norway) also describes rain right now; a "possible" doesn't count. I tested your three options on the 66 radar calls the live recorder caught at airports since 24 Sept. "A source rain vote" and "blended rain above zero" changed nothing, because every one of those calls already had both; in 9 of them the only rain word was Tomorrow.io's own. "Radar two hours running" threw away 21 of the 46 real rain calls. Asking for a rain word from a different source is the version that helps: right 74 % of the time instead of 70 %. It drops 5 dry calls, including Durban twice on 6 Oct over a clear sky, and also catches Johannesburg on 23 Sept. It drops 4 wet ones; 3 of those still show "Might rain", 1 (Gqeberha 4 Oct 07:10) shows Cloudy. **The trade you should know about:** the radar's founding case, Strand on 19 May, when it was raining and all four other sources said clear, would no longer say "Rain's here." No option on your list would have caught it either. The airport harness has no radar, so its scores don't move. Scorecard: `review/accuracy/results/radar-second-signal.md`.
