# PW — PAIRING TASTE PROFILE

**Status:** operational. Written from one complete labelled pass (set-001). Follow these rules on any future proposal run or meme-writer batch. Where a rule rests on a small sample it says so — treat those as leaning, not law.

**Evidence base:** `review/pairing-proposals.json` (1,470 proposals, 294 image×pool units) joined against Al's export (885 approvals across 292 of 294 slots). Overall approval **885/1470 = 60%**. Every rate below is real counts from that join.

---

## 0. The finding that changes how you work

**Al is judging the pair, not the line.** Of 214 lines proposed three or more times, **174 (81%) got different verdicts on different images.** The same line is a yes here and a no there.

> `witty:wind#15` "Every loose object in the garden has chosen violence." — **3/18.**
> **YES** on: *"The plastic patio chairs are in a heap on their sides on the lawn."*
> **NO** on: *"There are leaves flying past her at every height in the frame."*

So: **never rank lines by how good the line is.** There is no such thing as a strong line in the abstract. Rank by how hard the line and this specific frame lock together. A line that scored 4/4 elsewhere will still be rejected here if the lock isn't there.

## 1. The contribution test — apply to every proposal before you submit it

**Ask: does the line say something the picture cannot say by itself?** If the picture already says it, the line is dead, no matter how well written.

This single test explains most rejections. Compare three lines that all mean "the sky is impressive":

| Line | Rate | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `storm#54` "Midnight and the sky is showing off." | **5/5** | Adds *midnight* — a fact you cannot see in the frame. |
| `night#19` "The sky looks like it's showing off." | **3/3** | Used on quiet star fields — characterising something the eye reads as still. |
| `storm#16` "The lightning is putting on a proper show." | **1/13** | Used on 13 lightning photographs. The bolt already is the show. Zero contribution. |

More of the same failure:

> **REJECTED** `clear#3` "Not a cloud in sight. Not one." — my note: *"More than half the frame is empty blue above the deck — not a single wisp in it."* I was proud of the match. The match **was** the problem.
> **REJECTED** `cloudy#28` "Nothing to see here. Literally." — on *"An empty tar road curving to the sea… nothing else in the frame at all."*
> **REJECTED** `fog#0` "Can't see a thing. Not a thing." — on *"The only readable objects in this entire photograph are two red tail lights and one streetlight."*

And what contribution looks like when it lands:

> **APPROVED 4/4** `rain#25` "The traffic is emotional." — supplies a feeling to a picture of tail lights.
> **APPROVED 4/4** `cold#6` "The heater is your best friend. Your only friend." — supplies loneliness to a picture of a heater.
> **APPROVED 4/4** `storm#58` "Every flash is a photo nobody takes in time." — supplies a human failure to a picture of lightning.
> **APPROVED 3/3** `cold#97` "The last log is a decision, not an inventory item." — supplies scarcity and stakes to a picture of a fire.
> **APPROVED 4/4** `fog#14` "The streetlights are trying their best." — supplies effort and sympathy to a picture of fog.

**Rule: the frame supplies the fact. The line supplies the feeling, the consequence, the off-screen fact, or the human motive. Never the fact again.**

## 1a. The swap test — Al's ruling, 2026-08-18, and it outranks the hook rules below

From the bespoke pass: **171 of 196 kept — 87%** — against 60% for the pass-1
bank. Al's reason for the rejections, in one sentence:

> "The ones I rejected are because they are trying to find something funny in the
> image and lose sight of the actual weather."

**The test: move the line to a calm, mild day. If it still works, it is out.**
The weather has to be the thing that makes the line true — not the setting a
joke about a person, an object or a household happens to stand in.

| Rejected | Why it fails the swap |
|---|---|
| "Two glasses on that table and only one person came outside." | Works on any evening. |
| "That is a two-person job and there is currently nobody in the garden." | Works for any fallen trampoline. |
| "The school run has an extra step today and she finds it hilarious." | A school-run joke with weather as scenery. |
| "Somebody is going to have to close that door eventually." | Any open door, any night. |
| "The headlights behind him have been gaining for the last kilometre." | A traffic line; the wind causes nothing in it. |
| "The umbrella came down hours ago and never went back up." | About an umbrella, not about the heat. |

And the same frames' survivors, which cannot be moved:

| Kept | Why it survives |
|---|---|
| "The leaves came in. The cat did not go out." | The wind put the leaves inside. |
| "Somebody is going to remember that washing at about two in the morning." | Only the wind puts that thought in your head. |
| "That hair was a decision made indoors about forty minutes ago." | The wind undid it. |
| "The sparks are going sideways and the tongs are still going in." | Sideways sparks are the wind. |
| "Bo-Kaap in the last light, and the wind is doing the housekeeping." | The wind is named as the agent. |

**Second species of the same failure: the RIGHT weather, wrong bucket.** Two
rejects on wind frames were sunset and dryness lines — "Friday evening, and
Signal Hill has taken the last of the sun", "That curtain has been dry since
about four o'clock". Weather is present; it is not *this* weather.

**This refines §4's "borrow a non-weather human system" (70%), it does not undo
it.** Borrow the traffic, the office, the neighbours — but the weather must
still be the cause inside that frame. `rain#25` "The traffic is emotional" (4/4)
works because the rain caused the traffic. "The headlights behind him have been
gaining" does not, because the wind caused nothing in it.

**Corrected 2026-08-18, after the ruling was audited.** The raw export reads
173/206 = 84%, but two of the 42 images had their briefs transposed — the bin-day
lines were written for the suit photograph and vice versa — so 8 rejections and
2 keeps on that pair are evidence about a mix-up, not about this rule. Excluding
them: 171/196 = 87% overall, wind 80%. Every example in the tables above is from
an unaffected image; two earlier ones were not, and were replaced.

Where this bites hardest: **wind**. In the bespoke pass wind kept 80% while
heat kept 91% and storm, rain and clear kept 100%. Wind is the condition where
a frame offers the most human comedy — chased hats, blown washing, ruined hair —
and therefore the condition where it is easiest to write the weather out of the
joke.

## 2. Match the line's internal picture, not just its topic

A line carries its own small image. That image must match the frame's, at the level of shape and stage — not just subject.

**Pose vs thrashing.**
> `wind#6` "The trees are doing involuntary yoga." — **2/11.**
> **YES** both times on a *single palm bent into a held curve*: *"The palm is bent into a curve with every frond swept to one side and held there."*
> **NO** on every "many trees all bending" frame: *"The gums behind the garden are thrashing with every branch pointing one way."*
> Yoga is a pose: singular, still, held. Thrashing is not a pose.

**Aftermath vs motion.**
> `wind#15` "Every loose object… has chosen violence." — **3/18.** All three YESes show objects *at rest, displaced* (a slop on the grass, a chair on its side, chairs in a heap). All 15 NOs show things *in flight* (leaves, blossom, napkins, sparks). The joke is about aftermath; it needs aftermath.

**Individual vs shared.**
> `cold#9` "Blanket burrito mode: activated." — **3/6.**
> **YES**: *"completely encased in a heavy wool blanket with only the head out"*; *"built an entire cave out of the duvet."*
> **NO**: *"One blanket pulled across all four laps"*; *"sharing one thick blanket across both laps."* A burrito is one person, enclosed.

**Mass vs texture.**
> `rain#11` "Rain so heavy it should pay rent." — **5/9.**
> **YES** where volume shows as accumulation: *"the whole road already under"*, *"a solid curtain the length of the stoep"*, *"the entire pool surface is boiling."*
> **NO** where rain shows as pretty texture: *"every individual streak coming down through the cone of the street lamp"*, *"streaking right across the neon."*

**Before you submit a proposal, name the shape the line implies (a pose / a heap / one wrapped body / a mass of water) and confirm that exact shape is in the frame.**

## 3. What kind of hook earns approval

Measured on the reasoning I wrote for each proposal:

| Hook | Rate |
|---|---|
| Line names an object literally present in frame | **81/120 = 68%** |
| A person doing something specific (verb + body) | **260/409 = 64%** |
| Sky / light description only, no subject | **231/419 = 55%** |

**Anchor to a thing or a body, not to the sky.** The sky-only penalty is worst exactly where the sky is most spectacular — because that's where redundancy bites:

| Condition | sky-only hook | hook with a subject |
|---|---|---|
| storm | **22/51 = 43%** | 73/124 = 59% |
| wind | **26/56 = 46%** | 58/119 = 49% |
| cloudy | 22/43 = 51% | **89/132 = 67%** |
| fog | 28/48 = 58% | **70/92 = 76%** |
| clear | 27/40 = 68% | 87/135 = 64% |
| cold | 28/41 = 68% | 90/134 = 67% |

On **storm and wind, always anchor to a person, animal or object.** On **clear and cold**, a sky-only line is fine — those skies are quiet enough that a line still contributes.

## 4. Line-style rules with measured rates

**Longer lines win. This is the most counterintuitive result — do not "tighten" copy.**

| Length | Rate |
|---|---|
| 6–7 words | **267/495 = 54%** |
| 8–9 words | 239/387 = 62% |
| 10–11 words | 139/217 = 64% |
| 12–14 words | **108/143 = 76%** |

The winners nearly all carry a second beat that turns:
> `cold#74` "Wet sea cold: the kind that gets through four layers and a good mood." — **3/3**
> `cold-clear#10` "The kind of day Joburg pretends it doesn't have until you're standing in it." — **3/3**
> `wind#50` "Somebody forgot to bring in the washing. The washing has not forgotten." — 2/3

Short and punchy is what dies: `weekend#16` "No alarm. No agenda. Just vibes." — **2/15**.

**Named places beat generic SA flavour. Flavour slang actively loses.**

| | Rate |
|---|---|
| Named SA place (Joburg / Karoo / Table Mountain / Highveld / Boland…) | **40/53 = 75%** |
| Concrete SA noun (braai, bakkie, stoep, hadeda, windpomp, rusk) | **44/61 = 72%** |
| Neither | 813/1340 = 61% |
| **Flavour slang** (boet, lekker-as-decoration, kuier, vibes, nè) | **28/69 = 41%** |

Slang is fine when it is *the joke's mechanism*, dead when it is seasoning:
> **APPROVED 3/3** `cold#19` "You're not cold, you're 'lekker koud'. Big difference." — the Afrikaans word is the whole gag.
> **APPROVED 1/1** `rain#45` "Petrichor is the Greek word for it. Our word is 'sjoe'." — same.
> **APPROVED 2/2** `storm#0` "Jislaaik, stay inside!"
> **REJECTED 0/1** `weekend#0` "Braai weather, boet! No excuses." — *boet* is decoration.
> **REJECTED 1/10** `weekend#13` "Kuier weather. Get the people together."
> **REJECTED 0/2** `weekend#4` "Weekend vibes so strong they need their own playlist."

**"Vibes" is close to a kill-word on its own:** `weekend#16` 2/15, `weekend#4` 0/2, `cold-clear#45` 0/2, `fog#35` 1/3. The exceptions are ironic uses (`fog#2` "Silent Hill vibes…" 3/3, `cloudy#9` "Grey vibes. The sky matched my Monday." 1/1).

**Borrow a non-weather human system.** Lines that route the weather through traffic, the office, admin, money, school, family or neighbours: **100/143 = 70%** vs 785/1327 = 59% without. `rain#25` "The traffic is emotional" 4/4; `clear#62` "All meetings moved to the shallow end" 3/3; `cold#97` "…not an inventory item" 3/3; `wind#51` "Tomorrow morning's first job: a search party" 3/6.

**Stop instructing the reader.** Imperative openers: **64/122 = 52%.** Exhortations (go / get out / do X): **57/100 = 57%.** Both below the 60% baseline, and the worst offenders cluster here — `wind#39` "Close it, tie it down, surrender" 2/9, `storm#29` "Unplug the things. The sky has plans." 1/8, `storm#17` "Windows closed, kettle on, and wait." 2/6.

Imperatives survive in one case only: **when the object of the command is physically in the frame and not yet used.**
> **APPROVED 1/1** `weekend#15` "The tongs are calling. Answer them." — two pairs of tongs in shot.
> **APPROVED 2/2** `weekend#10` "The coals aren't going to light themselves." — coals in shot.
> **APPROVED 3/3** `weekend#24` "The braai is the social calendar. Show up." — four people already round a brick braai.

## 5. Per-bin and per-condition notes

**Approval by condition:** fog 70% · cold 67% · clear 65% · cold-clear 64% · cloudy 63% · rain 58% · storm 54% · heat 52% · **wind 48%**.
**Approval by bin:** fog 70% · clear 68% · cold 67% · night 66% · cold-clear 64% · cloudy 63% · heat 59% · rain 58% · storm 54% · **weekend 48% · wind 48%**.

### weekend bin (46/95 = 48%) — the split is sharp and usable
The bin is not weak. It is **two bins wearing one name.**

- **Braai-object lines score near-perfect *when the object is in frame*:** `weekend#3` "Beach or braai? Yes." 3/3 · `weekend#24` 3/3 · `weekend#15` 1/1 · `weekend#10` 2/2 · `weekend#14` "The Weber's been waiting all week" 1/1 · `weekend#1` "Fire up the Weber. It's the law." 1/1 · `weekend#7` "Call the mates. Get the meat. Let's go." 2/2.
- **Generic weekend-mood lines are the worst-performing family in the whole set:** `weekend#16` 2/15 · `weekend#13` 1/10 · `weekend#12` "Your out-of-office is doing the heavy lifting" 1/5 · `weekend#4` 0/2.

**Rule for the weekend bin: propose a braai/Weber/tongs/coals/beach line only when that object is visible; otherwise reach for `weekend#8` "Today's plans: exist outside." (11/13) or `weekend#11` "Shoes optional. Attitude mandatory." (4/6), which are the only mood lines that carry.**

`weekend#8` also shows the boundary: **YES** wherever a household or group is visibly outdoors and the indoors is visibly empty (*"Nobody is inside that house"*); **NO** on a solitary figure in a confined outdoor space (*"She has come out onto the balcony… and stayed there"*).

### wind bin (84/175 = 48%) — kill the metaphors, keep the named and the literal
Every wind line that reaches for a writerly metaphor loses: `wind#38` "Everything lightweight has ambitions tonight" 1/8 · `wind#15` 3/18 · `wind#6` 2/11 · `wind#25` "Free microdermabrasion on the promenade" 1/4 · `wind#32` "The sea applauds, the wind takes a bow…" 2/5.
The two perfect scorers are named and literal: **`wind#2` "Table Mountain's tablecloth is out." 4/4** (Table Mountain in frame with cloud on it) and **`wind#14` "The wind has a personal vendetta against umbrellas." 4/4** (an actual destroyed umbrella in frame).

### night bin (23/35 = 66%) — highest-trust bin, keep it plain
Perfect or near-perfect: `night#7` "The moon's doing the most tonight" 2/2 · `night#19` 3/3 · `night#1` "Perfect night to actually see the Milky Way" 2/2 · `night#13` "Not a cloud. Just you and the Southern Cross" 2/2 · `night#11` "The neighbourhood cats have taken over" 1/1 · `night#16` "Tomorrow's weather is tomorrow's problem" 2/2.
Weakest: `night#12` "Perfect weather for pretending you'll go to bed early" 2/5 · `night#18` "Warm enough to leave the window open. Brave enough?" 1/3 · `night#5` "Dark outside, bright tomorrow" 0/1.
**Night wants plain observation and a named sky object. It does not want a bit.**

### storm (54%) — do not scold the competent
`storm#18` "Not the time for hero moves, hey." — **4/9.**
**YES** where the frame shows retreat or comic defeat: destroyed umbrella; dad carrying the dog and a child indoors; two people flat on the floor coaxing a dog out.
**NO** every time the frame shows someone doing hard work well: *"hauling a tarp over hay bales while it is already coming down on him"*; *"walking towards the water with a board under his arm."*
**Rule: the app never mocks a person doing their job or taking a real risk competently. Aim the joke at the weather, at objects, at animals, or at the reader's own comfort — not at a working farmer.**

### heat (52%) and rain (58%)
Heat's losses are mostly redundancy on the obvious (`heat#28` "Slowly becoming a puddle in the corner" 1/5, `heat#6` "The AC is begging for mercy" 1/4, `heat#24` "Your steering wheel is a hot plate" **0/3**). Rain's losses are the same shape (`rain#14` "Everyone's forgotten how to drive. Again." 1/5, `rain#23` "The puddle was deeper than it looked" 1/5, `rain#32` "The pavement is a river and the river has plans" 3/13).

### Flagged images
Images I flagged as weak/mismatched scored **32/60 = 53%** vs 853/1410 = 60% clean — a real but modest penalty. **Both zero-approval slots were flagged-or-pool-mismatched cases:** `clear|dawn|A|sun` (frosted winter vineyard routed to the braai-forward weekend bin — all five weekend lines rejected) and `wind|day|B|thu` (hat-chasing frame; all five wind lines rejected). **When a slot's pool genuinely cannot serve the frame, say so instead of proposing five losers.**

## 7. Never do

1. **Never caption the condition.** If the line's content is "it is foggy / hot / windy / raining", it is dead on arrival.
2. **Never propose a line because it scored well before.** 81% of repeated lines flipped verdict. Judge the pair every time.
3. **Never use "vibes" as the payload.** Only as irony against a named thing.
4. **Never sprinkle slang for flavour** — *boet*, *lekker*, *kuier*, *nè* as decoration score 41%. Use them only when the word *is* the joke.
5. **Never issue an instruction whose object isn't in the frame.** No "unplug the things", "close it, tie it down", "get the people together" over an image with no plug, no latch, no gathering.
6. **Never mock someone working or taking a real risk competently** — the farmer with the tarp, the surfer, the construction crew. Aim at weather, objects, animals, or the reader's own comfort.
7. **Never tell the reader what to feel** ("Beautiful and terrifying in equal measure", "The prettiest lie you'll see all day"). Give them the mechanism; they'll do the feeling.
8. **Never tighten a line to sound punchy.** 6–7 words = 54%, 12–14 words = 76%. Cut vagueness, not length.
9. **Never name a landmark, region or object that isn't in the frame** — no Helderberg on a Table Mountain shot, no *bakkie* on a sedan, no Karoo on the Midlands.
10. **Never fill five slots when the pool genuinely can't serve the frame.** Propose what works and flag the gap.
