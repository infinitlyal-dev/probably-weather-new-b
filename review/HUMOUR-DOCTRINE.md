# PW — HUMOUR DOCTRINE
### Written from Al's 349 hand-placed pairs (`review/set-001-line-matches-ruled.json`), 2026-08-30

**Method:** 39 of Al's placements viewed as pairs — photograph and line together — spread
across all nine conditions plus the weekend/night/uv/rain-possible cross-bins, including
his own authored pair (`rain#custom-1`). Phrased as writing instructions for the finishing
run. `PAIRING-TASTE.md` still binds underneath this; where this doc is silent, that one
rules. Where the corpus refined a taste-doc rule, this doc says so.

---

## The spine, verified

**Every line is a claim about the weather. The photograph is the evidence for the claim.**
Not decoration, not a scene the line describes — evidence. The strongest placements in the
corpus never mention the funniest thing in the frame:

- Egg frying on a bakkie bonnet at dawn → *"6am and the sun is already done warming up.
  That wasn't a warm-up, that was a warning."* The line is pure meteorology. The egg is
  the proof, and it is never named.
- A man water-skiing down a flooded suburban street (Al's own line, his own placement) →
  *"Clouds are crying like NZ at the 23 Rugby WC!"* The line converts the rain into a
  national victory. The skier — the loudest joke in the whole library — goes unmentioned.
- Golden retriever flat in the kiddie pool, two kids displaced → *"Too hot to argue. Too
  hot to function."* The dog is never named. The heat is.

**Test every draft against this:** strip the photo away — the line must still be about
weather. Put the photo back — the photo must prove the line true, and preferably prove it
absurdly.

## The ten instructions

### 1. The funnier the photo, the drier the line
Al pairs deadpan copy with absurd frames. The image punches; the line underplays. Never
explain the photo's joke, never compete with it. On the slip-slops-into-the-office frame
the winning line is *"When the weather behaves this well, so do we. Roughly."* — one dry
word ("Roughly") carrying the whole dress-code violation. If your line is wackier than the
photograph, start over.

### 2. Name an object only to launch the joke somewhere else
Objects literally in frame (hadeda, braai, fridge, trampoline, washing, wipers, dog) are
gold as anchors — but the joke must leave the object immediately: off-screen, forward in
time, or into feeling.
- Trampoline resting against the wall → *"Someone's trampoline is now two streets away."*
  (extends it off-screen)
- Frost on windscreens at night → *"Tomorrow's first task: five minutes of windscreen
  scraping in your work clothes."* (extends it forward in time)
- Naming the object to describe it is the caption-competition failure that killed pass 1.

### 3. The weather is a character; the household has dignity
Anthropomorphise freely — it is the corpus's favourite engine. The sun is on airplane
mode, on a tea break, bunking. The wind has opinions, makes house calls, does the
housekeeping. The moon hangs a do-not-disturb sign. Clouds stay for supper. Cars
surrender, cough twice, deserve a wash. Animals get full personhood: the dog *booked* his
spot under the bed; the sausage dog wears his jersey *with dignity*; the chair is taken,
*rules are rules*. Write the weather with intentions and the objects with feelings.

### 4. Two beats; the second turns
The corpus's signature shape is statement + turn:
- *"Most beautiful time of day. Say the people who stay inside."*
- *"The washing is not going to dry. We hang it up for hope."*
- *"Full sunshine, full jacket. Both are non-negotiable."*
- *"Winter sun: all of the light, none of the heat. Nice try, sun."*
Do not tighten a line into one punchy beat — 12–14 words at 76% keeps vs 54% for 6–7
(taste doc §4) held in this corpus. Cut vagueness, never length.

### 5. Tease the reader; side with the worker
Reader-directed jabs are everywhere and welcome: *"Outside is a test of character. You're
failing."* / *"Your hands are in your pockets and they're not coming out."* But every
working person in frame — the dawn fishermen, the Karoo farmer, the mother scraping frost
— is treated with respect or quiet solidarity; their competence is never the punchline.
Aim mockery at the weather, at objects, at animals (affectionately, siding with them), or
at the reader's own comfort. Never at a person doing their job.

### 6. Write the line for its hour and its day
Al's placements are time-true: 6am lines on dawn frames, *"7pm and still 30 degrees"* on
dusk, fridge raids and *"Tomorrow's weather is tomorrow's problem"* at night, Monday lines
on Monday slots, braai lines on weekends with the braai in shot. The zeros list skews
dusk/night — write lines that live at that hour (supper, sundowners, tomorrow looming,
sleep), not day lines wearing a dark background.

### 7. SA specifics carry; decoration dies — and the brag is allowed
- **Named places** when in frame or honestly implied: Karoo, N1, Upington, Cape Doctor,
  Table Mountain's tablecloth. A plausible everyman brand is fine (*"The Toyota started
  first try"* on a generic frosted sedan).
- **Slang only as mechanism**, never seasoning: *"lekker koud"* as the joke itself,
  *"boet"* as the address in a reprimand whose object is visible (*"Your high beams are
  making it worse, boet"* — beams blazing in frame). Vibes/boet/kuier as decoration remain
  kill-words.
- **The national brag is house voice.** *"This is the weather we brag about overseas."* /
  Al's NZ rugby line. Patriotic cheek, current references, even an exclamation mark —
  sparingly (one in 380).
- **Eskom/load-shedding: closed.** Al hand-placed the two existing lines (`storm#12`,
  `storm#5`) — they stand as his ruling, but write no new ones.

### 8. Extend the frame — forward, sideways, or inward
The line supplies what the camera cannot: the future (*"Sleep tight, dams. You worked hard
today."*), the off-screen (*"Your car is out there. Somewhere. Probably."*), or the
interior monologue (*"It looks lovely out there. We've chosen to believe it from in
here."*). If the line only supplies what the camera already shows, it is dead (taste doc
§1 — still the single biggest killer).

### 9. Each condition has a register — write inside it
Verified against the corpus:
- **clear** — celebration and the brag; permission to be smug. Sky-only lines allowed here.
- **cold-clear** — the dissonance bucket: looks lovely, is freezing; sun without heat;
  windscreen scraping; believe it from inside. Never state the dissonance outright.
- **cold** — negotiation with comfort: heaters, blankets, the dog's chair, breath effects.
- **heat** — negotiation with relief: fridge, pool, shade; the Karoo making statements.
- **wind** — agency and displacement: what the wind *did*, moved, took, decided. Aftermath
  shapes need aftermath frames, motion shapes need motion (taste doc §2 held perfectly —
  Al placed "involuntary yoga" on the single held-curve palm and "chosen violence" on the
  at-rest trampoline).
- **rain** — traffic, dams, the mortality of umbrellas, plans dissolving; joy allowed.
- **storm** — the pecking order, dogs under beds, unplug-the-TV tradition; the reader
  watches from inside. Lightning-narration only where the bolt is background to a domestic
  scene, never where the bolt is the whole frame.
- **fog** — the world misplaced: streetlights trying their best, the neighbourhood eaten,
  the road existing on faith.
- **cloudy** — honest mediocrity: the 6/10 date, Tupperware lid, sky on mute; the
  half-and-half sky IS the app's own "probably" — self-reference welcome here.
- **night** (bin) — plain observation, a named sky object, no bit.

### 10. Run the two mechanical checks before submitting anything
1. **Swap test** (taste doc §1a): move the line to a calm mild day — if it survives, out.
2. **Shape test** (taste doc §2): name the shape your line implies (one bent palm, a heap
   at rest, one wrapped body, accumulated water) and confirm that exact shape is in frame.

## What this run must not do

1. No new Eskom/load-shedding lines (the two placed ones are grandfathered by Al's hand).
2. No captioning the condition or the frame's headline fact.
3. No mocking competence; no grim misery — every frame stays bearable-to-joyful.
4. No slang decoration; no "vibes" as payload.
5. No instructions whose object is not in shot.
6. No landmark/object naming that the frame doesn't show or honestly imply.
7. No telling the reader what to feel — give the mechanism, they do the feeling.
8. No writing wackier than the photograph.
9. No forcing lines onto a frame the register can't serve — flag the gap instead
   (taste doc §7.10 stands).
